# Positions: Iman Gadzhi

Specific stances on key business decisions with dates and supporting evidence from 44 sources.

## Pricing
High-ticket only. Minimum retainer for any B2B client service should be $1,500 to $2,500 per month, scaling up to $10,000+ per month as results prove out. Competing on low price attracts the most difficult clients and destroys margins.
- Quote: "way you're paying for the retainer and that way you're bring paying for the value that we" (S004) [2021-06-19]

## Discounting
Never discount your core retainer. If a client balks at price, negotiate on scope, add upfront bonuses, or offer a performance revenue-share component, but keep the cash floor intact.
- Inferred from S004, S010.

## Raising Money (Venture Capital)
Strongly against VC and outside angel money for early-stage founders. Venture capital saddles founders with external board pressure, loss of equity, and forced growth at the expense of profitability. Self-fund through high-margin client services.
- Inferred from S001, S003, S026.

## Debt
Avoid consumer debt and speculative leverage completely. Debt is only justifiable when purchasing proven, cash-flowing digital or physical assets where the cash yield easily covers debt service.
- Quote: "that, the most passive kind of investment there is, like sometimes it'll still, you know, mentally plague" (S037) [2025-06-18]

## First Hire
Hire a specialized contractor for service delivery (e.g., media buyer or copywriter) before hiring sales reps or account managers. The founder must lead sales while delegating fulfillment.
- Quote: "services, or whether you're going to use a contractor. And that's okay. Here's what I did when" (S010) [2025-11-18]

## Firing
Fire quickly when integrity, work ethic, or baseline standards are breached. Keeping an unaligned operator on the team toxifies culture and slows execution.
- Inferred from S008, S012.

## Partners & Equity
Avoid 50/50 early-stage equity partnerships with friends. Bring on operators with clear milestone-vested equity or revenue-share agreements. Protect your ownership fiercely.
- Inferred from S001, S003.

## Focus vs Diversification
Strict monomania until $1M to $5M in net profit. Do not launch a podcast, merchandise line, or second agency while your primary engine is under $50k/month. Diversify only once cash flow is institutionalized.
- Quote: "same style. Service delivery, copy and paste, offer, same offer. When you have multiple niches, it's literally like" (S010) [2025-11-18]

## Side Projects
Kill side projects ruthlessly. Side projects are almost always subconscious distractions from the uncomfortable work of sales and outreach in your main company.
- Inferred from S018, S023.

## Niche Selection
Choose niches with high transaction values and immediate advertising budget (e.g. luxury e-commerce, med spas, real estate developers, high-ticket consultants). Never target broke businesses or struggling local stores.
- Quote: "one is all about Niche selection in simple terms what type of businesses do you want to" (S011) [2025-03-05]

## Content Marketing
Long-form YouTube content is the highest-leverage medium for building deep institutional trust with an audience. Short-form clips are useful only as top-of-funnel discovery to drive viewers into long-form education.
- Quote: "that is, whether you like it or not, you have a personal brand. Whether that's your 300 followers on an Instagram" (S032) [2025-02-25]

## Paid Advertising
Do not run paid ads until your offer has been validated through direct outreach and organic conversion. Once validated, paid ads should be used to pour fuel on a proven fire.
- Inferred from S010, S011.

## Personal Brand
Every founder in the 21st century has a personal brand whether they manage it or not. Curating an intentional media presence gives you distribution leverage across every business you will ever launch.
- Quote: "that is, whether you like it or not, you have a personal brand. Whether that's your 300 followers on an Instagram" (S032) [2025-02-25]

## Artificial Intelligence
Use AI as a massive force multiplier for lean operational leverage: research, initial copy drafts, coding assistance, and client audit generation. Never use AI to flood prospects with low-grade generic spam.
- Inferred from S035, S010.

## Remote Work
Strong proponent of completely distributed, location-independent teams. Centralized physical offices introduce massive overhead, geographical talent restrictions, and corporate theater.
- Quote: "espresso addiction, not for some swanky office so that we can look cool so we can just" (S004) [2021-06-19]

## Work Hours & Burnout
Burnout does not come from working 14-hour days; it comes from chaotic disorganization, lack of tangible results, and toxic habits. Work in intense, dedicated Monk Mode sprints followed by structured downtime.
- Quote: "monk mode really isn't about balance it is about immersion it is about guaranteeing success and when I" (S018) [2022-01-27]

## Selling the Company
Build a business to generate cash flow, not solely to flip to an acquirer. If you exit, do so on your own terms when you have higher-leverage software or media vehicles ready to deploy that capital into.
- Quote: "thought okay when are we going to sell it for a billion and how do we make" (S001) [2022-12-06]
- Quote: "I'm shutting my agency down Iman godzi an entrepreneur has developed his Empire" (S014) [2023-01-01]

## Metrics That Matter
Cash collected in the bank account, net profit margin, and client retention rate. Ignore vanity metrics such as follower counts, gross transaction volume, or investor valuation headlines.
- Inferred from S001, S004, S026.

## Competition
Do not obsess over competitors. In service and education businesses, there is virtually unlimited demand for elite operators who actually care about delivering client outcomes.
- Inferred from S004, S010.

## Timing
Take immediate advantage of generational technological and market shifts (e.g. social media advertising in 2017, AI automation in 2024-2026) before playbooks become saturated.
- Inferred from S010, S026, S035.
